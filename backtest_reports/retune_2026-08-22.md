## Evidence reviewed: what the 33 out-of-sample trades actually did

The out-of-sample exit mix of the chosen combination is stop 9, take-profit 3, momentum-shift 21, trailing-stop 0.
Two facts stand out and were investigated directly rather than assumed.

### 1. The trailing stop is structurally inert on this data

The trailing stop arms at +1.0R of favourable excursion.
Across all six swept combinations of the Gate 1 run, and across every exit variant tested here, the trailing-stop exit count is exactly zero.
Measured favourable excursion explains why: of the 21 out-of-sample momentum-shift trades, zero ever reached +1.0R of favourable excursion before exiting, and the mean best-excursion was only 0.276R.
The trail cannot fire on trades that never build the profit cushion it needs to arm.

### 2. The momentum-shift exit is protective, not premature

For each of the 21 out-of-sample momentum-shift exits, price was followed forward from the bar after the exit with the trade's own original bracket left untouched (original stop, original take-profit), under the same conservative worst-of-intrabar rule the engine uses (a stop touch wins a same-bar tie).
This is the honest counterfactual for "was the exit premature": if the trade had simply been left alone, what would it have done?

- Out of sample (n=21): if held, 17 of 21 would have gone on to hit the original stop for a full loss, and only 4 would have reached take-profit.
  The realized momentum-exit average was -0.069R; the hold-the-bracket counterfactual average was -0.456R.
  Cutting these trades on the momentum shift saved roughly 8R across the 21 out-of-sample trades versus naively holding.
- In sample (n=48): if held, 31 of 48 would have hit the stop and 17 would have reached take-profit.
  The realized average was +0.055R; the hold-the-bracket counterfactual was +0.035R, essentially a wash.

So the momentum-shift exit is neutral-to-slightly-helpful in sample and strongly protective out of sample.
It is not cutting winners short; it is bailing out of trades that would otherwise ride down to a full stop.
The out-of-sample trades simply do not move into profit (mean favourable excursion 0.276R), which is an entry-quality signal, not an exit-parameter defect.

## Hypotheses tested

Two hypotheses were formed from the evidence and tested as real parameter changes, each re-run through the full Gate 1 sweep on the same data and split.

- H1 (the optimistic clue): the 10/20/50 two-vote majority flips too early in a trend and cuts trades before target.
  If true, requiring more agreement before exiting (holding longer) should improve the out-of-sample result.
  Test: `ma_exit_min_votes = 3` (unanimous).
- H2 (the alternative the counterfactual supports): the exit is protective and the entries are the weak link, so the useful lever, if any, is exiting sooner to cut losers faster, or arming the trail earlier to bank the small excursion.
  Tests: `ma_exit_min_votes = 1` (any single MA flip); a faster ensemble `ma_ensemble_periods = (5, 10, 20)` at two votes; that faster ensemble at one vote; and `trail_activation_rr = 0.5`, `trail_distance_rr = 0.5`.

Reading the table:

- H1 (hold longer) makes both the in-sample and out-of-sample results slightly worse, exactly as the protective-exit counterfactual predicts.

  The momentum-shift exit has priority over the trail in the decision core, and these trades reverse before the trailed stop is reached, so lowering the arm threshold changes nothing on this data.

## Structural signal-count limit (a separate finding, not an exit problem)

Exit parameters cannot change how many entries fire: `n_signals` is 124 for every variant, unchanged, because entries are computed before any exit logic runs.
The out-of-sample closed-trade counts are 33 (lookback 45), 9 (lookback 60), and 4 (lookback 75), against the PRD 6.1 target of roughly 150 to 300 out-of-sample signals.
Reaching that target would need on the order of five to ten times more signals than the whole 3-year, 14-symbol window produces.
This is a structural limit of universe size times reachable Bitunix history times the first-touch, one-zone-one-trade cadence, and no exit-parameter change can lift it.
It is the same finding the Gate 1 real run recorded (PRD 6.2); this investigation confirms it is orthogonal to the exit parameters.

## Conclusion

Keep `ma_ensemble_periods = (10, 20, 50)`, `ma_exit_min_votes = 2`, `trail_activation_rr = 1.0`, `trail_distance_rr = 1.0`.
Changing any of them to a value that produces a bit-for-bit identical failing out-of-sample result, while claiming an improvement from a fractional in-sample wiggle, is precisely the curve-fitting conduct rule 7 exists to prevent.
The honest result of this task is negative: the exit parameters are not the reason Gate 1 fails.
The two real constraints are entry predictiveness (out-of-sample trades barely move into profit) and the structural signal-count shortfall, both outside the exit-parameter scope of this task and both requiring a captain decision (universe expansion, an entry-side redesign, or accepting the strategy does not clear Gate 1 as specified) rather than a unilateral exit-parameter edit.
